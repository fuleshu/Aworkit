# Canonical history retention: what is kept, what is tombstoned

Normative model for retention in the local semantic history store, decided 2026-10-05 from a measured live profile. Retention may release a payload's heavy bytes. It may never release the Chat, the conversation, the index or the authority.

## The measured problem

The local history store had no retention. The store file measured 31,552 MB on disk and the event payload inside it totalled 15.0 GB. Two event kinds are approximately 97% of that payload:

| Event kind | Events | Payload |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `context.checkpoint` | 7,694 | 7,506 MB |
| model-call `span.started` | 7,923 | 7,507 MB |

Both kinds store the same turn's context twice, adjacent in sequence, up to 2.9 MB each on ~800k-token contexts. Everything else is small: tool data 31 MB, internal non-sidebar streams 14 MB, and 482k `span.content_delta` events at 197 MB.

The same profile carried a second complete copy of that payload in the delivery queue. `delivery_outbox` held 573,690 records, every one already delivered, and 15,523 MB. The queue was therefore roughly half of the 30.8 GB store, and no retention rule had ever looked at it. The delivery queue's own rule is stated under The delivery queue below.

Separately, 29 of 153 Chats had been tombstoned (deleted in the UI) and their events were still stored.

## Governing decision

Retention may release a payload's heavy bytes. It may never release the Chat, the conversation, the index or the authority.

Every rule below follows from that one constraint. Retention is a payload operation, not a history operation: it edits what a record carries, never whether the record exists, where it sits in sequence, or what it is allowed to decide. The delivery queue is the one record kind retention removes outright, and it does so only for records that have already been delivered.

## Kept: never a prune candidate

1. Every Chat index entry - created, selected, pinned and deleted - so nothing vanishes from the sidebar.
2. The conversation: user and assistant messages and cards.
3. Tool call records, their results, usage and timing, so the whole Chat stays visible.
4. Per Chat, the newest `context.checkpoint` of every `(ownerKey, nodeId, child)` scope.
5. The newest 20 turns of checkpoints per scope.
6. The newest 20 model-call span inputs.
7. Everything the stream head needs to continue.
8. The newest 512 `delivery_outbox` records, delivered or not.

Items 1 to 3 are kept unconditionally and are not candidates for any release pass. Items 4 to 6 are the retention floor for the two candidate kinds. Item 7 is the continuation requirement that bounds items 5 and 6: a scope never retains fewer turns than the head read needs. Item 8 is the delivery queue's floor, and it is fixed at 512 records rather than derived from a retention window.

## Released: the two payload candidates

Exactly two event kinds are payload candidates: `context.checkpoint`, and `span.started` whose payload `spanKind` is the model-call kind. No other kind is a candidate at any size, including the 482k `span.content_delta` events, tool data, and internal non-sidebar streams.

Within those two kinds, the only release candidates are superseded payloads: superseded `context.checkpoint` payload snapshots, and superseded model-call `span.started` input bodies. A payload outside the retention floor is a candidate; a payload inside it never is.

Payload release is not the only retention act the store performs. Already-delivered `delivery_outbox` records are removed, not tombstoned, and no other record kind is removed at all.

## Disposition by event kind

| Event kind | Kept or released | Heavy field | What remains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chat index entry (created/selected/pinned/deleted) | Kept - not a candidate | none | The complete index entry, so the Chat stays in the sidebar |
| Conversation (user/assistant messages and cards) | Kept - not a candidate | none | The complete conversation record |
| Tool call record, result, usage and timing | Kept - not a candidate | none | The complete tool record, so the whole Chat stays visible |
| `context.checkpoint`, newest of every `(ownerKey, nodeId, child)` scope | Kept - not a candidate | `snapshot` | The intact `snapshot` |
| `context.checkpoint`, within the newest 20 turns of its scope | Kept - not a candidate | `snapshot` | The intact `snapshot` |
| `context.checkpoint`, superseded | Released - tombstoned | `snapshot` | Event id, kind, sequence, timing and usage; every payload field except `snapshot` |
| `span.started` with payload `spanKind` = model-call, among the newest 20 inputs | Kept - not a candidate | `input` | The intact `input` |
| `span.started` with payload `spanKind` = model-call, superseded | Released - tombstoned | `input` | Event id, kind, sequence, timing and usage; every payload field except `input`, including `hasInput` |
| `span.started` with any other `spanKind` | Kept - not a candidate | none | The complete event |
| `span.content_delta` (482k events, 197 MB) | Kept - not a candidate | none | The complete event |
| Other event kinds (tool data 31 MB, internal non-sidebar streams 14 MB) | Kept - not a candidate | none | The complete event |
| `delivery_outbox` record, among the newest 512 | Kept - not a candidate | the copied event payload | The complete record: its `delivery_cursor`, its `delivered` flag and its copy of the announced event |
| `delivery_outbox` record, already delivered and older than the newest 512 | Removed - the row is deleted | the copied event payload | Nothing of the row: its identity and cursor are gone with it, and the event it announced is still in `semantic_events` |

## The form of a release: a tombstone, not a delete

The event is not removed. It keeps its event id, kind, sequence, timing and usage. Its payload keeps every field it had except the heavy one - `snapshot` for a checkpoint, `input` for a model-call span - which is removed outright. In its place the payload gains one object under the key `prunedPayload`:

```
{
  "schemaVersion": 1,
  "kind": "context_checkpoint" | "model_call_input",
  "bytes": 0,
  "digestBefore": "sha256:<JCS/RFC-8785 canonical SHA-256 of the released value>",
  "prunedAt": "<timestamp>",
  "retainedTurns": 20,
  "reason": "superseded: only the newest snapshot per context scope and the newest turns of a Chat keep their bytes"
}
```

There is no separate top-level `pruned: true` field and no separate top-level timestamp or reason: the presence of the `prunedPayload` object is itself the marker. A tombstone is recognized because it carries that object and no longer carries the heavy field - the omission is stated in the record itself, and a reader can tell that a value was released, when, and of what size without consulting anything outside the store.

`hasInput` is deliberately preserved on a model-call span, because the call did carry a request; the marker is what says its bytes are gone. The released value is digested before it is released, so `digestBefore` identifies exactly which bytes are gone. `kind` names the released value: `context_checkpoint` for a checkpoint `snapshot`, `model_call_input` for a model-call span `input`. `bytes` is the byte length of the released value, `prunedAt` the release timestamp, `retainedTurns` the retained-turn count of the scope, and `reason` the stated cause.

The delivery queue does not use this form. A delivered record has no consumer left and carries nothing worth keeping, so it is deleted rather than tombstoned.

## The delivery queue: delivered records are removed, the newest 512 stay

The store also holds `delivery_outbox`, the delivery queue between the trusted core and its presentation port. Each row carries a copy of the event it announced. Measured on the real profile: 573,690 rows, every one already `delivered = 1`, holding 15,523 MB - 7,507 MB of `context.checkpoint` copies and 7,513 MB of model-call `span.started` copies. That was a second complete copy of the history payload and roughly half of the 30.8 GB store.

Retention removes already-delivered records and keeps the newest 512 records. Four facts fix that boundary.

1. A delivered record is never read again. `pending_outbox_v1` is the only reader and selects `delivered = 0 AND delivery_cursor > ?`.
2. The uncertain-commit verification never reaches a delivered record. `verify_durable_commit`, the path that quarantines a store it cannot verify, re-checks only the batch being committed at that moment, whose records are still undelivered.
3. The newest records must stay, or a later insert would reuse a cursor. `insert_outbox` derives the next `delivery_cursor` from the queue's maximum, and `delivery_outbox_cursor` is UNIQUE.
4. The newest 512 records are also the grace period for an acknowledgement that is still in flight, because acknowledging a record that is gone is an `UnknownOutbox` error that the drain propagates.

A removed record leaves no marker. Its identity and its cursor are gone with it, and the event it announced is still in `semantic_events`, so nothing that was announced becomes unreadable.

## The write path: the automatic bound during an append

Without waiting for the explicit action, a Chat bounds itself as it works. When an append adds a checkpoint or a model-call input, the pass examines only the windows that append moved, walks the warm committed view newest first, and releases at most 4 superseded payloads per append, over a bounded look-back of 4,096 events.

Truncating that look-back is safe by construction. Every event it never reached is older than every event it decided about, so a truncated scan can only leave a displaced payload for the next append to decide. It can never release a payload still inside the retention window, and never the newest snapshot of a scope.

Only a complete warm view is used, and a release is applied to the in-memory view as well as to the store, so the next append cannot decide the same release twice. A failure there is never reported to the caller and can never end a Run.

## The legacy migration: a released tombstone is not a payload difference

`initialize_history_index` re-runs the legacy migration on every open. It reads the legacy stream `chat.local` and requires each Chat migrated out of it to match its segment. The legacy stream and the migrated Chats are separate streams with separate retention windows, so a release tombstones one side and not the other. Payload equality therefore refused the whole profile with "partially migrated Chat history differs from its legacy source", and the application would not open.

The migration now tolerates a payload difference where either side is a released tombstone. The kind must still match, and any other difference is still refused rather than silently rebuilding the Chat.

This is why a payload-level change to stored history must be followed by opening the real profile and projecting every Chat: the migration invariant is enforced on open, and only a real open proves that a release left every migrated Chat matchable.

## Per Chat and per scope, never global

Retention is per Chat and per scope, never global. A scope that runs alongside another cannot be starved by it, and the newest checkpoint of every scope is always kept, because that is the exact record a resumed pass reads. The retained-turn count is computed per scope, not across the Chat, so a Chat with many scopes retains the floor for each of them rather than sharing one budget.

## Resumption contract

The newest intact checkpoint per scope, plus the newest 20 turns, is the resumption authority. A reader that finds a tombstone where a source used to be must state the omission and continue; it may not fail a Run.

A fork or replay started from an intermediate turn may no longer find that turn's exact snapshot. It must report that explicitly rather than inventing context. Fork or replay from the stream head is unaffected, because the head's records are always inside the retention floor. This is the accepted, documented tradeoff of releasing superseded snapshots.

## Concurrency and integrity

A release rewrites payload bytes only, in one transaction, under the store's cross-process exclusive maintenance lease. Sequence numbers, `chat_streams` heads and the history index are untouched. A payload release therefore cannot reorder history, move a head, or change what is pending delivery. The delivery queue is bounded by its own rule and is never touched by a payload release.

A Chat the user deleted keeps its index tombstone and loses its events, plus the `attempts`, `checkpoints` and `delivery_outbox` rows that reference its stream head, in one transaction. The index tombstone survives deletion precisely so the deletion itself remains visible.

### Foreign keys and the order a purge must respect

`semantic_events`, `attempts`, `checkpoints` and `delivery_outbox` each carry `FOREIGN KEY (chat_id, branch_id) REFERENCES chat_streams`. Removing a deleted Chat's stream head therefore requires removing its `semantic_events`, `attempts`, `checkpoints` and `delivery_outbox` rows first, in one transaction. A pass that deleted only the events and the head failed on the 19th deleted Chat with `FOREIGN KEY constraint failed`.

## Returning space to the operating system

Rewriting payloads frees pages inside the file; only `VACUUM` returns them to the operating system. Before the pass the store measured 31,552 MB on disk with the freed payload still inside it. `VACUUM` needs free disk roughly the size of the file, so the pass that releases bytes and the operation that returns them are separate steps with separate resource requirements. After the pass the file measured 2,349 MB, with no free pages and no write-ahead log.

## The explicit action

Settings shows the measured store size, the payload total, the superseded-snapshot pool, the delivery records and the deleted-Chat bytes, plus the kind breakdown, and it states the policy. "Reclaim space" reports what it can release before it starts, is confirmed by the user, emits progress for its two phases - releasing superseded payloads per Chat, then rewriting the store file - and never runs at startup or on its own.

## Cost

The explicit pass is user-confirmed and never runs at startup. A full pass over a 15.0 GB payload is CPU-bound because each released value is digested, so the action reports what it will release before it starts and runs off the interactive path. The automatic per-append pass is not that full pass: it is bounded to 4 releases over a 4,096-event look-back.

## Measured outcome

The pass was run on the real profile, and the measured store before and after it is:

| Measure | Before |
| --- | --- |
| Store file | 31,552 MB |
| `semantic_events` payload | 15.0 GB |
| `context.checkpoint` payload | 7,506 MB |
| Model-call `span.started` payload | 7,507 MB |
| `delivery_outbox` | 15,523 MB over 573,690 records, every one delivered |
| Chat streams | 140 |

| Measure | After |
| --- | --- |
| Store file | 2,349 MB |
| `semantic_events` payload | 2,015 MB over 597,746 events |
| `delivery_outbox` | 512 records holding 12 MB, with the maximum cursor preserved |
| Chat streams | 133 |
| Tombstoned payloads | 13,326 |
| Free pages and write-ahead log | none |
| The 29 deleted Chats | Events and `attempts`/`checkpoints` rows released, index entries kept |

The retained-snapshot floor is deliberate and is not free. The two superseded kinds still hold 1,662 MB by design, and that is the price of every Chat keeping its newest snapshot of every context scope plus its newest 20 turns.

Verification on the real profile: the store opens, and all 124 indexed Chats project with a timeline. Every Chat that has any checkpoint (68 of 68) still has at least one intact one, and every Chat's model-call window is exactly 20.

## Acceptance and verification

A fixture Chat with 50 checkpoints across 3 owner scopes and 50 model-call inputs must keep exactly the newest checkpoint per scope plus the newest 20 turns intact, byte-identical, and tombstone every other payload. The conversation, tool records, usage and timing must be unchanged. Continuing the Chat at its head must still work. A head fork must still work.

A fixture delivery queue with more than 512 already-delivered records must end with exactly the newest 512 present, the maximum `delivery_cursor` preserved, and a subsequent `insert_outbox` succeeding. The real-profile measurement above is the acceptance evidence for the whole pass, not a fixture result.

## Non-responsibilities

This policy does not decide the layout of the Settings surface that reports it, the exact wording of a reported omission, the retention floor for kinds other than `context.checkpoint` and model-call `span.started`, or the internals of the store-file rewrite beyond its being the second phase of the explicit action. It decides only what is kept, what is released or removed, the tombstone record that states a release, and the resumption guarantees a reader may rely on.
